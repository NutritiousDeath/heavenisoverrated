About
The Story

Music didn't arrive as a choice — it was already there. A grandmother at the piano. A grandfather with a banjo in one hand and a trumpet in the other. A mother who played violin. A father who played guitar. It was the language the family spoke before words.

Heaven is Overrated started in 2000 — one person, a fascination with strings, and whatever could be captured with an amp, a mic, and a cassette tape. The sound was raw and searching, more feeling than form, built from whatever was on hand and whatever felt right.

The Partnership

In 2005, Andrew met Randy Foss — known in darker circles as Baron Von Blutwurst. What followed was a creative wildfire: four albums recorded in thirty days, released on Reverbnation. The music had a partner, a momentum, a pulse it hadn't had before. Then a move across state lines pulled the duo apart, and Heaven is Overrated became a solo project once again.

Evolution

What started with cassette tapes evolved through FL Studio and Cubase — the tools changed, but the instinct stayed the same. The sound grew heavier, more orchestral, more layered. Symphonic metal with no vocalist, just the instruments telling the story.

In 2025, a friend named Russ from the band Solarset introduced DistroKid, and Heaven is Overrated went from local recordings to global distribution. Three releases followed in 2026: Symphony of Sparks (three years in the making), Ashes of Ascension (two years of obsessive craft across fourteen tracks), and NovaFide.

Yes, the drums are AI. No, the rest isn't.

Every string. Every note. Every arrangement. Human hands, human ears, human heart.

Legacy
Where It Started
